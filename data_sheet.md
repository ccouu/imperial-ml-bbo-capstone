# Datasheet: Function 8

## Function Overview
This datasheet describes **Function 8**, which simulates the real-world scenario of tuning an **8-dimensional machine learning model** (e.g., learning rate, batch size, regularization, layers). The objective is to maximize a validation accuracy score strictly bounded between `0` and `1`. The initial dataset provided a sparse, uniform seed of 20 to 40 observations, representing extreme sparsity for an `8D` volume.

## Nature of the Data
The input structure is an `N x 8` array of hyperparameter coordinates confined within a `[0, 1]` hypercube, returning a `1D` array of scalar performance scores. Over the weekly iterations, the dataset evolved from broad, space-filling exploration to highly clustered sampling around a specific high-yield region. Unlike other benchmarks, Function 8 proved to be largely **deterministic and exceptionally smooth**. Cross-validation diagnostics ultimately revealed that the entire `8D` landscape perfectly mapped to a **concave, second-degree polynomial manifold** without aleatoric noise spikes.

## Optimisation Strategy
The initial approach utilized an **Ensemble Regression-Kriging** architecture paired with an **Upper Confidence Bound (UCB)** acquisition function. Standard Gaussian Processes typically fail in `8D` due to the curse of dimensionality, so Regression-Kriging was deployed to capture the macro-slopes of the space using a parametric baseline. 

Exploration was aggressively forced in early rounds by setting the UCB exploration factor (`kappa`) between `2.0` and `3.0`. Once diagnostics confirmed the global polynomial structure with near-zero Mean Squared Error (MSE), `kappa` was decayed to `0.0`. The strategy then executed a critical pivot: abandoning stochastic Bayesian sampling entirely in favor of **bounded L-BFGS-B deterministic gradient optimization** calculated directly on the fitted polynomial pipeline, effectively turning a *black-box heuristic search* into a *white-box analytical problem*.

## Data Handling and Preprocessing
To strictly enforce parameter boundaries during analytical solving, all inputs were processed through a `MinMaxScaler` to maintain `[0, 1]` constraints. The surrogate model utilized a two-tier structure:
*   **Base Regressor:** `Polynomial Linear Regression` utilizing Scikit-Learn's `PolynomialFeatures(degree=2)`.
*   **Residual Model:** A standard `Gaussian Process`.

Target outputs were stabilized using a **sign-preserving logarithmic transformation** to prevent covariance matrix conditioning failures. Because the response surface was smooth and bounded, no major outliers required manual intervention.

## Weekly Iteration and Learning
New data points quickly confirmed that the vast `8D` search space was a highly structured, simple geometric shape rather than a chaotic landscape of local optima. Early **boundary probes** (evaluating the corners of the hypercube) were the most informative, quickly establishing global gradients and allowing the polynomial regressor to lock onto the correct curvature. 

To ensure the solver did not get trapped in high-dimensional saddle points, the analytical step utilized **30 multi-start random restarts**. All optimization paths converged to the identical absolute peak. In hindsight, implementing automated multi-degree polynomial hypothesis testing by Week 3 would have allowed this function to be solved analytically much earlier in the campaign.

## Performance and Results
The pipeline achieved a near-perfect output score of **~0.997**, securing **1st place** on the benchmark leaderboard. This value was produced by the exact coordinate vector generated by the `L-BFGS-B` solver in the final submission. Confidence that this represents the absolute global maximum is **100%**, as the cross-validation MSE of the polynomial surrogate was practically zero, and the deterministic solver found the exact mathematical peak of that verified surface. While the official documentation warned of a "complex" function where "global optimization is hard," applying the correct inductive bias revealed an elegantly simple underlying mechanism.

## Ethical, Practical, and General Considerations
This optimization task perfectly mirrors automated hyperparameter tuning for Large Language Models (LLMs) or deep neural networks, where evaluating every combination is financially impossible. The **Ensemble Regression-Kriging** strategy scales exceptionally well to these expensive industrial problems by pairing an aggressive global trend estimator with a localized GP to extract maximum information from minimal queries.

However, a limitation of this synthetic function is that real-world deep learning loss landscapes are rarely clean, unimodal polynomials; they often contain massive flat plateaus and steep cliffs. The primary risk for a future user analyzing this specific function is **over-engineering**—applying a complex Deep Neural Network surrogate to this `8D` problem would result in severe overfitting on the sparse sample budget, entirely missing the simple geometric reality of the landscape.
